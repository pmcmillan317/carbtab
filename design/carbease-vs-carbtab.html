CarbEase vs CarbTab

CarbEase was the one name the panels proposed that is worth a real look. Here it is run through the same test as the shortlist: the linguist, the ad exec, and the eleven panels re-voting, this time with only two names on the table.

The seam, first, since that is the thing your wife flagged

Neither name has the CarbPal problem. CarbTab hands a hard b to a hard t, two different parts of the mouth, so the join is crisp. CarbEase goes one better on paper: b into a vowel, no consonants touching at all, nothing to collide.

But CarbEase trades the collision for a blur. There is no hard sound to mark where "carb" ends and "ease" begins, so said quickly it can land as car-BEES or Carbies. That matters more than usual here, because Carbie and Carbi are both existing carb-tracking apps. A name that a listener might spell as "Carbies" can send your own word of mouth to a competitor.

Head to head

CarbEase

said carb-EEZ
Linguist
Cleanest possible seam. The high "ee" vowel does feel light and quick, which is on message. But the word boundary is not audible, so it drifts toward "Carbies," and "ease" has several plausible spellings from hearing it (ease, eaze, E's).
Ad exec
A promise, not a description. "It's easy" is warm and it speaks directly to someone who feels behind. But benefit-claim names are a worn wellness pattern, the word paints no picture for a logo, and the app now has to feel easy every single session or the name works against it.
Best room
The newly diagnosed. "That is exactly what I need to hear right now."
Worst room
Clinicians ("I won't put a marketing promise on a handout") and second-language users (five spellings around one table).
Strong pull, soft name

CarbTab

said carb-TAB
Linguist
Crisp seam, one hard syllable, and the word boundary is unmistakable because the t marks it. Spells itself from hearing it. No competitor it can be confused with.
Ad exec
A concrete noun with three on-target meanings: a running tab you settle later, a tab of paper, keeping tabs on something. Makes a clean lowercase wordmark and a simple icon. Stakes out "the running total," which no one in the category owns.
Best room
Parents, on the handoff test. "Log it in CarbTab" is unambiguous over text or a phone call.
Worst room
None strongly against it. The veterans found it a touch plain but still picked it.
Nothing to fault

CarbEase won exactly the two rooms it was built for: people in their first months, and older adults just starting insulin. Both rooms said a version of "if the app tells me it's easy, maybe I can do this." That pull is real and it is worth taking seriously.

It lost the other nine on the same few points every time. The veterans and the athletes reject a comfort promise on principle. The clinicians will not recommend a name that makes a claim. The second-language panel and the screen-reader panel could not pin the spelling or the word boundary. And two rooms landed on problems specific to this name.

Two flaws that only CarbEase has

It can route people to a competitor

Heard once and typed later, "CarbEase" becomes "Carbies" for a fair number of people. That is the search term for Carbie and Carbi, two carb apps that already exist. No other name on the shortlist has a mishearing that lands on a rival.

"Ease" plus digestion is already a thing

"Ease" is the verb in a lot of gas and bloating relief branding. The teen panel got there in about four seconds. Minor, but it is a joke the name invites and CarbTab does not.

What CarbEase gets right

The seam genuinely cannot be slurred the way CarbPal is, so your wife's specific objection does not apply. And for the single most emotionally raw moment in this whole audience, the first weeks after diagnosis, no other name reaches out a hand the way this one does.

The real question underneath

This is not really CarbEase versus CarbTab. It is a question about who the app is mainly for.

If CarbWise is aimed first at people in their first months with diabetes, when counting is terrifying and a reassuring name might be the thing that gets the app opened, CarbEase has an argument, and you would accept the "Carbies" tax as the cost of that warmth.

If it is aimed at the broad carb-counting population, including the people who will use it for twenty years, the ones who weigh every meal, the clinicians who recommend it, then a name that makes no promise and cannot be confused with a competitor is the safer foundation. That is CarbTab, and everything about how the app is actually built, fast, honest about its sources, friendly to power users, points at that broader audience.

Verdict

CarbTab, 9 rooms to 2.

CarbEase is a better name than its ninth-place finish suggests, and if your gut says the newly-diagnosed are the heart of this product, it is a defensible pick. But it carries a flaw none of the others do, a mishearing that leads to a competitor, and it asks the app to keep a promise on every screen. CarbTab asks nothing, hides nothing, and reads clean to every room.

If you want the warmth of CarbEase without the risk: keep CarbTab as the name and let "carb counting, made easy" be the tagline. You get the reassurance in the place where a promise belongs, under the name, not baked into it.
