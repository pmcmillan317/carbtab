Naming the carb counter

Ten fresh candidates for the app, since CarbWise, CarbPal and GramWise are all taken. Each name is checked the way a naming studio would check it: a linguist for how it sounds and spells, an ad exec for how it sells, then eleven simulated panels of the people who would actually use it, each also asked what they would call it instead.

The CarbPal problem, stated plainly

Your wife is right, and there is a name for what she noticed. Carb ends on a b, a sound made by pressing the lips together. Pal opens on a p, the same lip gesture without the voice. Two nearly identical moves back to back, so English speakers merge them and say car-pal.

The same collision hits CarbPal, CarbBuddy and CarbMate (the m is another lips-together sound), and it softly hits CarbWise, where b runs into a lip-rounded w and drifts toward car-wise.

What flows instead: carb followed by a k (Cue, made at the back of the tongue), a t or d (Tab, Tag, Dial, made at the tongue tip), an n (Note), or a vowel (Echo). Different muscles, clean hand-off. Your instinct for CarbCue is sound. It has one real wrinkle, covered below, but the seam between the two halves is not it.

The ten, one by one

01

CarbTab

20.5 / 25

The safe, strong pick. Reads instantly as "keep a running total," sounds crisp, spells itself.

LinguistSaid carb-TAB. The hard b hands straight off to a hard t, made in a different part of the mouth, so nothing slurs. One stressed syllable, and no one mishears the spelling. The stress pattern is the same one behind Fitbit and Venmo.
Ad exec"Tab" carries a bar tab, a running amount you settle later, which is exactly what the app is. Also a tab of paper, a tab you keep on something. It looks sharp lowercase as carbtab.app. The only ghost is the old diet soda Tab, and that is faintly on theme.
clean seamself-spellingon message"tab" is common
02

CarbSum

20 / 25

More distinctive than CarbTab, with the same honesty. It literally sums your day.

LinguistSaid carb-SUM. The b into s is clean. The one risk: if the stress slides off the first syllable it can slur toward "car bum." Keep the weight on CARB and it holds.
Ad execA math tone is a real differentiator in a category full of "manager" and "buddy." It feels precise, a little clinical in the way that suits people dosing insulin. Almost certainly wide open on trademark and domain.
coined feelwide runway"car bum" if mis-stressed
03

CarbNote

18.5 / 25

The gentlest option. Low pressure, diary-like, good for a tool people reach for when anxious.

LinguistSaid carb-NOTE. The n gives a soft landing after the hard b. Very easy to say and to spell.
Ad exec"Note" is warm and unintimidating, but it is also everywhere: Evernote, Apple Notes, Google Keep. You would sit slightly in their shadow, and the word undersells the calculation the app does.
calm, unintimidating"Note" is crowded
04

CarbCue

17.5 / 25

Your pick. The best sounding name on the list, with one real cost to decide on with eyes open.

LinguistSaid carb-CUE. The repeated hard k sound at the front of both words is a genuine bit of music, and rare in this category. Two wrinkles. Said quickly, "CarbCue" collapses toward barbecue, so try it out loud a few times. And "cue" has three spellings for one sound (cue, queue, Q), which sends some people to the wrong search result.
Ad execThe barbecue echo is a liability on radio and in word of mouth, but it is also sticky and food-adjacent, and a confident brand could lean into it with a small flame in the logo and a line like "your cue to eat." "Cue" also means the prompt right before you act, which fits the moment: you check it, then you eat. It trademarks well as a coined compound.
alliterationshort, ownablesounds like "barbecue"cue vs queue vs Q

Gramwell

17 / 25

The soft, ownable outlier. It sounds like a trusted brand but does not tell you what it does.

LinguistSaid GRAM-well. Smooth, no collision, a pleasant falling rhythm. "Gram" keeps the unit up front, and "well" nods to wellness without shouting it.
Ad execIt could be a supplement, a bakery, a wellness coach. You would spend real money teaching people that it is a carb counter. The upside is no baggage, a likely open gramwell.com, and room to build the meaning from scratch.
no baggagewarmdoesn't say "carb"

CarbTag

16 / 25

Fine, and forgettable. Every food gets tagged with its number, which is true but not memorable.

LinguistSaid carb-TAG. Clean seam. But it is one small sound away from CarbTab, so if you ever showed both, people would mishear one for the other.
Ad exec"Tag" does not stake a claim. Price tags, hashtags, tag you're it. Nothing wrong, nothing that sticks.

CarbDial

Countwise

CarbEcho

The most premium sounding name here, and the one a lawyer would strike first.

The panels

Eleven simulated rooms, each a different slice of the real audience. Everyone saw the same shortlist (CarbTab, CarbSum, CarbNote, CarbCue, Gramwell), picked one, and was asked what they would call it instead.

Panel 1  ·  A mixed room

Seven people spanning age, diabetes type and tech comfort.

Maya, 29
Type 1 since she was 14. Pump, CGM, doses in seconds between the classes she teaches.

"I don't read the name, I tap the icon. So keep it short and don't make me explain it. CarbTab. 'What's on my tab today.' Done. CarbCue I would have to spell for people every single time."

Picks CarbTab
Rob, 44
Counts every meal for his daughter Ellie, 9, diagnosed last year. High stakes, low patience for cute.

"I want it to sound like a tool, not a toy. CarbSum sounds like it does math, and that is what I need at 6pm with a plate in front of me. CarbCue made my wife say 'barbecue?' and that is the whole problem again."

Picks CarbSum
Dana, RD, CDCES
Diabetes educator. Recommends two or three apps to patients and needs them not to be mistaken for keto apps.

"My worry with anything 'Carb-something' is patients thinking it is a low-carb lifestyle app. CarbSum and CarbTab both read as neutral counting tools. I would write either one on a handout. Not Gramwell, I would have to describe it."

Picks CarbSum

Type 2, started insulin this year. Counting carbs for the first time in his life. Not a big phone person.

02

The first ninety days

Four adults three weeks to three months past a Type 1 diagnosis, still learning to weigh food, still scared of getting it wrong.

They flinch at anything that sounds like a system to master. "CarbSum reads like another test I'll fail." CarbNote got the warmest response, "it's just notes, I can do notes." One person: "I want the app to feel like it's on my side, not grading me."

Counting for someone small

Three parents of Type 1 kids aged 4, 8 and 11, plus a grandparent who does Wednesday pickups.

The handoff test dominated. "I text the babysitter 'log it in CarbTab' and she finds it. CarbCue over text and she's googling barbecue recipes." The grandparent needs to say it once on the phone and have it land. Everyone rejected "buddy" and "pal" outright, "not for a disease my kid has forever."

Twenty years in

The clinic

Carbs for performance

11

Reads it aloud

Four people who navigate by screen reader or large text, plus one with a hand tremor.

Spoken pronunciation matters. "CarbTab" and "CarbNote" are read cleanly and can't be confused. "CarbCue" is a problem: VoiceOver and TalkBack can voice "Cue" and "Queue" identically, and "is it the letter Q" came up. In an app list read aloud, "CarbTab" is instantly identifiable.

CarbTab took seven of eleven rooms. It rarely won on enthusiasm; it won because it was the name nobody had a problem with. CarbSum owned the two most analytical rooms, the long-timers and the clinic, on its plain math tone, though the athletes flagged "carb some." CarbNote owned the two most anxious rooms, the newly diagnosed and older adults new to insulin, on feeling gentle rather than like a test. CarbCue got exactly one individual vote across roughly fifty people, and Gramwell got none, though both had pockets of "I like how it sounds."

Names the rooms proposed

Setting aside restatements of the shortlist, these came up unprompted. Quick checks only.

CarbEase  from the newly-diagnosed room

The reassurance angle, aimed straight at people who feel behind. Clean seam, easy to say and spell, no app found. Risk: "ease" is a soft, slightly generic wellness word, and it undersells the precision. Worth a proper check.

CarbClear  from the read-it-aloud room

Reads cleanly by screen reader and in a list, unambiguous spelling, and "clear" nods at the app's honesty about where numbers come from. Also surfaced in the earlier research pass with no app found. The b into k-l is fine. Strongest of the proposed set.

CarbCard  from the parents room

A carb reference card, the thing families already tape to the fridge. Concrete and on-message for the handoff use case. No app found. Downside: two hard k sounds plus the d-into-nothing ending make it a slight mouthful, and "card" hints at payments.

Carb Diary  from the older-adults room

Gentle and instantly understood, which is why that room reached for it. But it is two words, generic, hard to own, and "diary" undersells the calculation. A description more than a name.

CarbLog  from the clinic room

The obvious clinical choice, and already an app (Carb Log, on iOS, does almost exactly this). Ruled out.

"grams"  /  carbz  veterans / the group chat

Real sentiments, not usable names. "grams" cannot be owned, trademarked or searched for. "carbz" reads as unserious for a tool people dose insulin from, and the novelty spelling repeats the CarbCue problem.

Verdict

Eleven rooms, the linguist and the ad exec all point the same way. The order did not change from the first pass; the eleven panels made it firmer.

  1. CarbTab. The pick. It won seven of eleven rooms, and it won them by being the name nobody could find fault with: clean seam, spells itself from hearing it, reads right by screen reader, "running tab" says exactly what the app does, and it looks sharp as a lowercase wordmark. The lowest-risk name that still has an idea in it.
  2. CarbSum. Take this for a more clinical, math-forward tone. It owned the two rooms that matter most for credibility, the twenty-year veterans and the clinicians. Two cautions: keep the stress on "CARB" so it does not slur to "car bum," and know that the athlete room heard "carb some" as a fueling cue.
  3. CarbNote. Take this if the priority is feeling gentle. It won the two most anxious rooms, the newly diagnosed and older adults just starting insulin, precisely because it does not sound like a test.

From the rooms themselves, CarbClear and CarbEase are the only proposed names worth a real look, and only if a formal check clears them.

On CarbCue: across roughly fifty simulated people it drew one vote, and the reason was always the same, they could not spell it aloud for someone else. Your ear is still not wrong, it is the most characterful name on the list. If you love it, the honest path is to commit to the barbecue echo rather than fight it, build the wordplay into the icon and tagline, and accept a small permanent tax every time someone says it out loud. If that reads as a fun constraint, pick it. If it reads as friction, that is your answer, and the answer is CarbTab.

Availability. Each name was searched against the app stores and the diabetes and carb-counting category, and none turned up an existing app. That is a first pass, not a clearance. Before committing, check the Apple App Store and Google Play directly, run a USPTO trademark search in the software (009) and health-services (044) classes, and confirm the .com and .app domains. Names already in this space to steer clear of: Carb Manager, Carbs & Cals, CarbScanner, CarbsControl, Carbie, CarbMind, CarbWise (the .ai site and two apps on Google Play), Carb Log, Carb Counter, SNAQ.
